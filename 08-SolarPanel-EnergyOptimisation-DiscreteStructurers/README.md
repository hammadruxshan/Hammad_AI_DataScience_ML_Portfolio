# 08 - Solar Panel Energy Optimisation Using Discrete Structures
## Abstract

This project applies discrete mathematics concepts to optimise solar panel energy systems in Malaysia. Solar energy is an important renewable energy source, but high installation cost, inefficient panel placement, shading, and output limitations can reduce adoption and effectiveness. The project explored how mathematical modelling can be used to improve the affordability, efficiency, and reliability of solar panel systems.

The project used concepts from discrete structures, including geometric progression, the sum and product rule, and the inclusion-exclusion principle. These concepts were applied to calculate optimal solar panel angle, estimate how many panels could be installed, determine total energy output, calculate installation cost, and adjust effective output by considering overlapping factors such as shading and efficiency loss.

This project demonstrates mathematical reasoning, optimisation thinking, renewable energy analysis, and the application of theoretical computing concepts to a sustainability-focused problem. While it is not a programming-heavy project, it shows the ability to use structured logic and mathematical models to support real-world decision-making.

## Problem Statement

Malaysia continues to rely heavily on fossil fuels, while renewable energy adoption remains limited by cost, efficiency, and implementation challenges. Solar energy has strong potential, but users may struggle to determine the best panel angle, placement, output, and cost-effective configuration.

This project addresses the need for mathematical models that can help optimise solar panel systems and make renewable energy adoption more practical and efficient.

## Objectives
To optimise solar panel angle and placement using geometric progression.
To calculate the number of solar panels that can be installed in a given space.

To apply discrete mathematics concepts to a renewable energy problem.

## Methodology
1. Background Research
The project began by reviewing Malaysia’s renewable energy goals, fossil fuel reliance, and the role of solar energy.
2. Problem Definition
Key challenges were identified, including solar panel cost, placement, angle optimisation, and output efficiency.
3. Mathematical Concept Selection
Geometric progression, sum and product rule, and the inclusion-exclusion principle were selected as the main mathematical tools.
4. Solar Panel Angle Calculation
Mathematical reasoning was used to estimate optimal solar panel angle.
5. Panel Quantity Calculation
The number of panels that could fit in an installation area was estimated.
6. Energy Output Calculation
Total energy output was calculated based on panel quantity and output assumptions.
7. Cost Calculation
Total cost was estimated based on the number of panels and unit cost.
8. Overlapping Factor Adjustment
The inclusion-exclusion principle was used to account for overlapping efficiency loss factors such as shading.
9. Discussion and Evaluation
Results were discussed in relation to affordability, efficiency, reliability, and renewable energy adoption.

## Limitations
The project used mathematical assumptions rather than real-time solar sensor data.
Weather, seasonal variation, and location-specific sunlight intensity were not deeply modelled.
Actual installation constraints such as roof structure, maintenance, and regulation were not fully considered.
The model is simplified and would need engineering validation for real-world deployment.
Cost estimates may change depending on supplier, location, and policy incentives.
